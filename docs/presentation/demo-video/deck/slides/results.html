DARSHAN (about 70 seconds) Thanks, Vismay. Everything here is measured on 72 test conversations, 83 minutes of real conversational Hindi with real village and market noise. None of the test speakers were used for tuning. Our main measure is strict. A word counts as wrong if it is misheard, or if it is given to the wrong speaker. [Point to the bars, top to bottom.] This chart shows where the errors come from. The speech recogniser alone, on one clean voice: 17.7 percent. Add noise and overlapping speech: 29.7. Add fully automatic who-spoke-when: 49.5. That last step, telling apart two people who talk over each other in a noisy market, is the largest source of error. Our clustering, both the spectral clustering and the Gaussian mixture the proposal asks for, is written from scratch, and it comes within error bars of pyannote, the standard tool. [Point to the grey bar.] And diarization works best on the original recording, before separation touches it. The proposal's order, separating everything first, gets 55.8. Ours makes 11 percent fewer errors, and wins in 50 of the 72 conversations. (Leave the cards on the right for the audience to read; the clip used the time.) [NEXT SLIDE.]
